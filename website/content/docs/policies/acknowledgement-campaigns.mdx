Publishing is not the same as people having read it. An acknowledgement campaign asks named people to read a published document and sign that they have. This page shows you how to run a campaign, how recipients sign, and how to track progress.

## Why campaigns matter

The campaign's record is the evidence. When an auditor asks "how do you know staff read the policy", you answer with a list of names, dates and versions.

## Run an acknowledgement campaign

<Steps>
<Step title="Open the published document">

Go to **Policies & Documents** and open the published document.

</Step>
<Step title="Start a campaign">

On the **Workflows** tab, find **Acknowledgement campaigns** and choose **Start campaign**.

</Step>
<Step title="Name it and add a message">

The **Campaign title** is filled in for you; change it if you like. Add an optional **Message** that recipients see.

</Step>
<Step title="Choose who must acknowledge">

Under **Recipients**, choose individual people, whole roles or groups, and mix them as you need. The dialog splits recipients into **Reviewers** and **Approvers**; everyone you pick in either list is asked to acknowledge. Verity expands roles and groups to their current members.

</Step>
<Step title="Set a due date and send">

Set a **Due date** if there is one, and choose **Start campaign**. Each recipient gets a notification.

</Step>
<Step title="Track progress">

The campaign page opens. Track who has signed and who has not from there.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Sign a document you were asked to read

When someone asks you to acknowledge a document, it appears in your notifications and under the document icon in the top bar.

<Steps>
<Step title="Open the request">

Open it from your notifications or the document icon. The read-and-sign page shows the document next to the sign-off box, so nobody can sign without opening it.

</Step>
<Step title="Read the document">

Read it through. If you have a question or a note for the owner, add a comment.

</Step>
<Step title="Sign">

Type acknowledge to confirm, then choose **Acknowledge**. Typing the word is what makes it a signature, not a stray click.

</Step>
</Steps>

Your name, the time and anything you write are recorded on the audit trail.

## Track and close a campaign

The campaign page shows how many recipients have acknowledged out of the total, the due date, and each recipient as **Acknowledged** (with when) or **Pending**. Comments on the campaign page keep questions in one place, and you can tag people in them.

When the campaign is done, the person who started it chooses **Close campaign**. A document's campaigns are all listed on its **Workflows** tab, each marked **Active** or **Closed** with its progress.

## When the policy changes

When you save a new version, the significance you choose matters: a major change means people need to read it again, and that choice drives whether a new acknowledgement is expected.

Changing a published document's text sends it back through approval. Once the new version is published, start a new campaign so the record shows who has read the version now in force. See [Approval and publishing](/docs/policies/approval-and-publishing/).
