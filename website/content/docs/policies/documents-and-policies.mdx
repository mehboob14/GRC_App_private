**Policies & Documents** holds your written commitments: policies, standards, procedures, guidelines and charters. Verity keeps the text, the version history, who approved each version, and who has read and signed it. This page shows you how to create a document, edit it and map it to your controls.

## The life of a document

<Lifecycle title="The states a document moves through" stages={[{ name: "Draft", note: "Being written; only the authors need to see it" }, { name: "Needs approval", note: "Sent for sign-off and waiting on a decision" }, { name: "Approved", note: "Every required approver said yes" }, { name: "Published", note: "Live: the version people are held to" }, { name: "Expired", note: "Renewal lapsed; a published document past its review date reads Needs renewal" }, { name: "Archived", note: "Retired on purpose, kept for the record" }]} />

A published document is never deleted. It is archived, and the text stays readable, because an auditor will ask what your policy said during the period under review. To archive a document, choose **Archive** in its row menu in the register. It moves to the **Archived** tab.

<Callout type="warning" title="Before you click">

Archiving is final. An archived document can still be opened, but its content can no longer be edited. If you need an up-to-date version later, create a new document.

</Callout>

## Create a document

You have three ways in, all from the register:

1. **From a template.** Choose **Templates**. Verity ships a library of policy templates: pick one and you get a complete draft in your workspace's name, ready to edit. This is the fastest route and the one most teams should use.
2. **From scratch.** Choose **New document**, then **Author in-app**, for a blank document with the same details.
3. **Upload a file.** If your policy already exists as a PDF or Word file, choose **New document**, then **Upload a file**. Everything else (approval, publishing, acknowledgement) works the same way.

## Draft a policy from a template

<Steps>
<Step title="Open the templates">

Go to **Policies & Documents** and choose **Templates**.

</Step>
<Step title="Choose the template">

Tick the template you want, for example Access Control Policy. Each template shows how many SOC 2 criteria it serves and how many details are left for you to fill in.

</Step>
<Step title="Create the draft">

Choose **Create draft**. You now have a draft with its own code, for example POL-004, with your company name already filled in.

</Step>
<Step title="Set the owner">

In the **Owner** panel, choose **Assign**. The owner is the person accountable for the content, not necessarily the author.

</Step>
<Step title="Check the details">

Choose **Edit details** to set the **Classification** (internal, confidential and so on) and the rest of the document's details.

</Step>
<Step title="Fill in the blanks">

A banner counts the details still to fill in before the draft is ready to approve. Choose **Fill in** to open the editor, which lists them under **To fill in**.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Edit the text

Open the document and choose **Edit content** on its **Content** tab. The full-screen editor opens in its own browser tab.

<Screenshot name="document-editor" alt="The full-screen document editor, with a formatting toolbar, a side panel listing the details still to fill in, and the policy text with highlighted placeholders" />

It is a normal rich text editor: headings, lists, quotes, links and images. The side panel lists what is still **To fill in**, any **Optional text**, what was **Filled in for you**, and an **Outline** of the document. Two things are worth knowing.

- **Every save makes a version.** Choose **Save version** and say what changed. The **Version history** tab lists every version: **What changed** shows the changes each one made, and **Restore** brings back an earlier one as a new version. Nothing is lost by saving often.
- **A change is major, minor or patch.** You choose the **Significance** when you save. Major means people need to read it again, and that choice drives whether a new acknowledgement is expected.

<Callout type="warning" title="Before you click">

Changing the text of an approved or published document, by saving a new version or restoring an old one, returns it to **Draft** and clears its approvals. They were given against wording that is no longer current, so the document must be approved again before it is published.

</Callout>

## Map a document to controls

The **Mappings** tab connects the document to the frameworks and controls it satisfies. Choose **Link controls** and pick the frameworks and controls. That link is what lets a policy stand as evidence for a control.

When the draft is ready, send it for approval. See [Approval and publishing](/docs/policies/approval-and-publishing/).
