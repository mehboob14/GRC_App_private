Approval in Verity is tiered, and publishing follows automatically from the last approval. This page explains how tiers work, walks you through sending a policy for approval, and covers what happens once a document is published.

## How approval tiers work

A tier is one round of sign-off, and each tier can name people, roles or groups.

- Assign a **named person** and that person must decide.
- Assign a **role or group** and any one member of it can decide on behalf of all.
- More than one tier runs in order: tier 2 opens only when tier 1 has cleared.

A document has two tiers, **Tier 1: Reviewers** and **Tier 2: Approvers**. You find both in the right-hand column of the document's **Overview** tab.

## Send a policy for approval

<Steps>
<Step title="Give the document an owner">

Open the document. If the **Owner** panel shows Unassigned, choose **Assign** first. Reviewers and approvers can only be assigned once the document has an owner.

</Step>
<Step title="Choose who approves tier 1">

On **Tier 1: Reviewers**, choose **Assign**. Pick a person, a role such as Security Officer, a group, or any mix of them, and choose **Assign**.

</Step>
<Step title="Let the workflow start">

Assigning tier 1 starts the workflow and notifies the people in it. There is no separate send button. The document moves to **Needs approval**.

</Step>
<Step title="Add a second tier if you need one">

On **Tier 2: Approvers**, choose **Assign** in the same way. Its people are notified once tier 1 clears.

</Step>
<Step title="Approvers decide">

Each approver gets a notification and a dedicated review page. They read the document, type approve or reject to confirm the decision, and choose **Approve** or **Send back**. Their name, the time and any comment they add are recorded on the audit trail.

</Step>
<Step title="The document publishes">

When the last required decision lands, the document is published automatically.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="note" title="Give a reason when you send it back">

Sending a document back returns it to the owner as a draft, with your comment attached. Say why, so the next draft can answer it.

</Callout>

## Track an approval

The tier cards on the **Overview** tab show each tier as **Not assigned**, **In review**, **Approved** or **Rejected**, with each person's decision. The **Workflows** tab keeps the **Approval history**: how many people on each tier have signed off, and when the tier was decided.

Approvals waiting on you personally also appear under the document icon in the top bar.

## Publishing

Published means live: this is the version people are held to. You do not publish by hand. When the last tier approves, the document is approved and published in one step.

Publishing is not the same as people having read it. To ask people to read and sign, run an [acknowledgement campaign](/docs/policies/acknowledgement-campaigns/).

If you later change the text of a published document, it returns to **Draft** and goes through approval again. See [Edit the text](/docs/policies/documents-and-policies/#edit-the-text).

## Reviews and expiry

Policies need revisiting. A document's next review date shows in the register's **Next review** column. When that date passes on a published document with no new version, the register shows it as **Needs renewal**, and a banner at the top of the register counts the documents that need renewal.

An overdue document is not archived: the text is still live, it is just overdue. To bring it current, update the text and send it for approval again. Assigning tier 1 starts a fresh review.
